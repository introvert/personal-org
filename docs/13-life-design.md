# Life Design — The Part That Isn't a Business Outcome

> ⚠️ Like [`10`](10-people-and-relationships.md) and
> [`11`](11-money-and-enough.md), this is **scaffolding, not conclusions.** It
> asks the questions the rest of the repo is downstream of, and the answers are
> yours. It is the last document because it is the one everything else serves.

---

## The gap this closes

Read this repo back and the asymmetry is obvious: it knows your cost structure
to the dollar, your competitive position in detail, and your architecture by
service — and it knows almost nothing about **what you actually want a week to
feel like.**

That imbalance is not just a documentation gap. It's the same imbalance the
whole repo diagnoses: the business side gets rigor, the life side gets whatever
is left. A "life operating system" that is 80% business strategy has quietly
reproduced the problem it was written to fix.

**The four questions below are the ones the endgame document (#14) actually
depends on.** You cannot write a walk-away number without knowing what you're
walking toward.

---

## Question 1 — What does a good ordinary week look like?

Not a good year. Not a milestone. **A Tuesday.**

Most people can describe their goals and cannot describe the daily life those
goals are supposed to produce — which is how you end up achieving things that
don't improve the week.

_(Fill in — concretely. Hours, places, people, not adjectives.)_

- What time does it start, and what happens first?
- How many hours of actual work, and of what kind — maker, manager, owner?
- Who is in it?
- What happens in the evening?
- What is in it that has nothing to do with either company?
- What is in your current week that would **not** be in this one?

Then the diagnostic question:

> **How far is that from this week — and which single change closes the most
> distance?**

If the honest answer is "not that far," that is genuinely important information:
it means the problem is load and boundaries rather than direction, and
[`PLAN.md`](../PLAN.md) already addresses it. If the answer is "very far," the
strategy is aimed at the wrong destination and no amount of execution fixes that.

## Question 2 — Who are you when you're not running Nightwatch?

The question founders reliably postpone, and the one that most often ambushes
them.

Thirteen years is long enough that the company is not just what you do. It is
how you are introduced, what fills the day, where competence is felt, and a
large part of how you understand yourself. **A liquidity event removes all of
that at once, and usually faster than the money becomes real.**

This is not a reason to avoid an exit. It is a reason to know the answer first.

_(Think about, before the endgame document.)_

- If Nightwatch were sold and the earnout done, **what would you do on a Monday
  morning six months later?** If the answer is vague, that is the finding.
- Which parts of the work would you genuinely miss — the building, the problems,
  the team, being needed, the identity of "founder"? They are separable, and
  they don't all need the same replacement.
- **What has Formaviva been doing for you all these years?** Reading the two
  repos together, one plausible answer: it is where the craft, taste, and
  identity live that the SaaS business doesn't have room for. If that's right,
  it is not a side project — it is a load-bearing part of who you are, and the
  cap in [`07`](07-formaviva-adjustments.md) is protecting more than a codebase.
- Is there something you have wanted to do for years and never had room for?

> **A founder who knows what they'd do next negotiates better, exits cleaner, and
> recovers faster.** One who doesn't tends to either refuse good offers for
> reasons they can't articulate, or accept and then struggle badly afterwards.

## Question 3 — Where and how do you want to live?

Rarely examined because it feels fixed. Mostly it isn't.

_(Fill in.)_

- **Place.** Slovenia long-term? Somewhere else? Does anything about the current
  setup exist only because the business made it convenient?
- **Rhythm.** Is the ideal a steady balanced week, or intense seasons alternating
  with real gaps? [`04`](04-time-and-attention.md) assumes seasons — is that
  actually you, or an accommodation?
- **Work shape.** Do you want to run a company again, build something small
  alone, do craft work, invest, teach, or take a genuine break first? These are
  very different lives and the choice is usually made by default.
- **How much work is the right amount?** Not the minimum — **the amount that is
  actually satisfying.** For most people who build things, that number is not
  zero, and assuming it is zero is how post-exit years go wrong.

## Question 4 — What do you want the next thirteen years to have been?

The last thirteen produced a technically excellent product, a real data moat, a
scene brand people trust, and a distribution problem. That is a real record.

The question is not "was it worth it" — it's what the same span should contain
next, given that it is **the same amount of time again**, spent with more
resources, more skill, and less energy to waste.

_(Think about.)_

- What would make the next stretch feel well spent, independent of outcome?
- What would you regret **not** having done?
- What is currently absent that you'd want to be routine — health, travel,
  music, family, craft, a specific kind of work?
- What are you willing to be **worse** at in order to be better at those?

That last one is the whole repo restated at life scale. Subtraction was the
answer for two businesses; it is the answer here too. **A life with everything in
it is the same failure as a product with every feature.**

## How this connects to everything else

These four questions are not separate from the business plan — they are its
inputs, and currently they are the missing ones.

| This question | Determines |
|---|---|
| The ordinary week (Q1) | Whether the load in [`PLAN.md`](../PLAN.md) is a temporary sprint or the wrong life |
| Identity beyond the company (Q2) | The endgame document (#14) — what outcome you're actually optimizing for |
| Place and rhythm (Q3) | Which endgame scenarios are even attractive |
| The next thirteen years (Q4) | Whether "enough" ([`11`](11-money-and-enough.md)) is a number you can hold |

> **The endgame document cannot be written well without these.** That is the
> real reason it keeps getting deferred — it looks like a financial document, but
> it is actually this one wearing a spreadsheet.

---

## How to use this

Not in a Monday review. Not squeezed between demos.

**One afternoon, away from the desk, on paper.** Ideally before writing the
endgame document — or as its first half. It will take longer than it looks,
because the questions are simple and the answers are not.

Then revisit it once a year, in the annual review. The answers are allowed to
change. What matters is that they change **deliberately, in a quiet room** —
rather than being decided for you by whichever offer happens to arrive.
