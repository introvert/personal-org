# Quality of Life — What Actually Raises It, Ranked by Effort

_[`01-life-thesis.md`](01-life-thesis.md) defines what a good life means here.
[`PLAN.md`](../PLAN.md) ranks the actions by system value. This document answers
the third question: **of everything already on the list, what actually changes
lived experience — per unit of effort, and on what horizon?**_

> **This document adds no new actions.** It re-sorts the existing ones on a
> different axis and evaluates the stated priorities against each other. Where
> the two orders disagree, **`PLAN.md` governs execution sequence** — it accounts
> for dependency, which this document deliberately ignores. This one governs
> **what to protect when the week collapses.**

---

## How each item was evaluated

Four questions, no invented scores:

1. **QoL delta** — how much *lived experience* changes. Not business value.
2. **Effort** — to do it once, and to keep it.
3. **Latency** — days, weeks, or years until it is felt.
4. **Decay** — does it hold unattended, or must it be re-paid?

### The finding that orders everything below

**The highest-return quality-of-life items here are one-time policy changes, not
habits.** This follows directly from the energy audit in
[`02`](02-current-state.md): the drain is *unresolved open loops and continuous
partial attention* — _"unresolved questions are what actually exhaust people —
not work"_ — not workload. An unwritten cap is re-decided weekly. An unrouted
alert interrupts nightly. An unconfirmed number taxes every decision made on it.

A written policy costs once and pays forever. A habit costs daily and decays.
So the order is: **buy the policies first, then fund the habits.** Both matter;
only one is cheap.

## Tier B — The floor habits: cheap, highest return, must be re-paid

These are the only items whose effort recurs — and they are also the items
[`PLAN.md`](../PLAN.md) says to protect **above everything else** when the week
slips. That is not sentiment; it is because everything in Tier A and C degrades
without them.

| What | Why it earns its place | Cost to keep | Plan |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wake anchor + morning daylight** | The single highest-leverage item in the repo. Downstream effect on decision quality and on willingness to have hard delegation conversations exceeds any productivity system ([`03`](03-energy-and-health.md)) | Daily, near-zero | #13 |
| **One genuinely off day per week** | Most-skipped, most restorative. A "light" day does not count | One day | #13 |
| **Movement as calendar appointments** | Leftover time does not exist in this operating model and will not appear. 2–3 strength sessions + daily walks | 2–3 hrs/week | #19 |
| **Friday close (15 min)** | An un-closed week runs in the background for two days — this is what makes the weekend actually off | 15 min/week | #13 |
| **Eat and hydrate on a schedule** | _"Skipped lunches followed by late heavy dinners are a sleep problem wearing a food costume"_ | Zero, once habitual | #13 |

**Start at a volume that survives a bad week.** That is the only volume that
compounds — five items over three months, per the sequencing in
[`03`](03-energy-and-health.md), not an ambitious program in week one.

### Where this order and `PLAN.md` genuinely differ

One place, and it is worth naming rather than smoothing over: **testing the
payment flow is `PLAN.md` #1 and has near-zero quality-of-life delta.** It ranks
first there because money may be leaking right now. That is correct, and
`PLAN.md` wins the week. The lesson is only that the two axes are different —
not that either list is wrong.

## What does not raise quality of life here

Each of these is tempting, and each has already been ruled out with evidence
elsewhere in the repo. Listed so they are not re-adopted quietly.

- ❌ **More discipline.** The failure is structural, not motivational. Structure
  is what [`04`](04-time-and-attention.md) provides.
- ❌ **A personal productivity system.** Adopt, don't build — a half-built one
  becomes a third venture ([`09`](09-technology-leverage.md)).
- ❌ **An ambitious health program.** Abandoned in week three under an incident
  is worse than never started.
- ❌ **More hours.** Tried for 13 years. The distribution problem was never an
  hours problem.
- ❌ **Winning against a named competitor.** Competitive emotion is an energy
  leak with no revenue attached.
- ❌ **Proving Formaviva works as a business** to justify having loved it. It
  owes a cap, not profitability.
- ❌ **Another strategy document.** Both businesses already have correct
  strategies. **Including this one — if this file becomes a project, it failed.**

## How to use this document

- **When the week collapses:** protect Tier B first, then Tier A. Cut business
  items before personal ones — this inverts the current default, which is
  precisely why it is written down.
- **When something new is proposed:** it must beat something already in Tier A
  or B, and name what it removes ([`05`](05-decision-framework.md) Gate 3).
- **Quarterly:** re-read the priority table in the quarterly review
  ([`11`](11-review-cadence.md)). Change a rank only on evidence, and date the
  change. Delete a priority that is no longer true rather than carrying it as
  guilt.

_The one-line version: **buy the policies, fund the habits, protect the floor,
and let the good week be a consequence rather than a goal.**_
