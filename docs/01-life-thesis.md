# Life Thesis — Goals, Values, and What "Best Quality of Life" Means Here

_This is the top of the stack. Every other document in this repo is downstream
of it. If a business decision conflicts with this document, this document wins —
that is the entire point of writing it down before the decision arrives._

---

## Why this document exists

Both companies have a written strategy. Neither has a written statement of what
the person running them is optimizing for. That asymmetry is the source of most
of the drift: when the only written goals are revenue goals, every ambiguous
hour resolves toward revenue, and the unwritten goals lose by default — silently,
for years.

## The definition

**A best-quality life here is not a maximized life. It is a life with a high
floor, deliberate scope, and enough slack to enjoy what has been built.**

Three tests, in priority order:

1. **The floor test** — health, sleep, relationships, and financial security are
   not variables to be traded for upside. They are the floor. A quarter that
   grows MRR and lowers the floor is a failed quarter.
2. **The scope test** — can what is currently owned be run at a sustainable
   pace by the people actually available? If not, the answer is subtraction, not
   heroics.
3. **The enjoyment test** — is there a real, protected place for the work that
   is loved for its own sake (Formaviva, music, the scene) and for time that is
   not work at all? A life that only contains obligation has already failed,
   regardless of the P&L.

Stated as outcomes, with the horizon each belongs to. Deliberately few — a long
goal list is the same failure mode as a wide product surface.

## Values

The Nightwatch value set (`docs/01-company/mission-values.md`) is genuinely good
and largely transferable: Innovation, Taking Responsibility, Transparency,
Encouraged Growth, Exceptional Work, Performance, No Bullshit, Robustness.

**But it has a structural gap, and that gap has a cost.**

Every value in that list is an _expansion_ value. Innovation adds surface.
Exceptional Work ("we don't believe in good enough") raises the bar on every
surface added. Performance and Robustness raise the standard further. Nothing in
the set ever says _stop_, _fewer_, or _enough_. A value system with no braking
value predictably produces exactly what both repos document: three web
front-ends, four codebases, thirty strategy documents, two companies, one person.

So the personal value set is the company set **plus two counterweights**:

### 🚀 Innovation · 💪 Responsibility · 🔍 Transparency · 🌱 Growth · ⭐ Exceptional Work · ⚡ Performance · 🚫 No Bullshit · 🛡️ Robustness

_(carried over — these are real and they work)_

### ➕ Finishability
> **Finish before starting. A thing that is 80% done in three places is 0% done.**

The most expensive habit in both organizations is the started-but-unfinished
rewrite. Applies to code, strategy documents, business lines, and side projects
equally. Operationally: nothing new begins until something old is shipped,
sunset, or explicitly deleted — and "explicitly deleted" is a legitimate,
respectable outcome.

### ➕ Deliberate Smallness
> **Surface area is the enemy. Choose the smallest thing that could work, and
> defend it against your own ambition.**

This is the direct antidote to "big-company product surface on a small-team
budget." Before adding anything — a feature, a tier, a channel, a codebase, a
company — the question is not "is this good?" (it usually is) but **"what is
being removed to make room for it?"** If the answer is "nothing," the answer is
no.

### ➕ The Floor Before the Ceiling
> **Protect health, sleep, relationships, and runway before optimizing upside.**

Explicitly ranks the floor above growth. This is the value that lets you decline
a legitimately good opportunity without it feeling like a failure of ambition.

## The decision hierarchy

When two things conflict, resolve in this order. This is the whole point of
having written values — it makes hard calls mechanical instead of exhausting.

```
1. Health & safety        — non-negotiable, never traded
2. Integrity & promises   — customers, artists, team, family
3. Financial floor        — runway and security, personal before corporate
4. Focus                  — the one committed thing beats the interesting new thing
5. Growth & upside        — pursued only from a stable position of 1–4
6. Craft & elegance       — genuinely loved, deliberately ranked last,
                            because ranking it higher is what caused the problem
```

**Craft last is not an insult to craft.** It is the recognition that craft is
the strongest instinct here and therefore the one that needs the tightest rein.
Craft is the reward for having done 1–5, not the substitute for them.

## Anti-goals — things explicitly not being optimized for

Naming these prevents the slow re-adoption of goals never actually chosen.

- ❌ **Being the biggest.** €10M ARR is a plan, not an identity. A €3M business
  with a real team and a life is a better outcome than a €10M business that
  consumes the decade.
- ❌ **Winning against Peec, Bandcamp, or anyone by name.** Both repos already
  concluded this: Peec is a tailwind, Bandcamp cannot be out-Bandcamped.
  Competitive emotion is an energy leak with no revenue attached.
- ❌ **Rewrites as a form of progress.** The most seductive failure mode
  available. Modernization for security: yes. Rewrites for elegance: no.
- ❌ **Proving Formaviva "works" as a business** to justify having loved it.
  It does not owe anyone profitability to deserve existence. It owes a cap.
- ❌ **Heroic hours as a strategy.** They have already been tried for 13 years.
  The distribution problem was never an hours problem.

## How to use this document

- **Weekly:** does the coming week's plan violate the decision hierarchy? Fix
  the week, not the hierarchy.
- **Quarterly:** re-read the goals. Delete any that are no longer true rather
  than carrying them as guilt.
- **At every fork:** the question is always _"which option raises the floor?"_
  — not _"which option has the higher ceiling?"_ The ceiling has never been the
  binding constraint here. The floor has.
