# Time & Attention — The Weekly Operating System

_How the week is actually shaped, so that the priorities in
[`01-life-thesis.md`](01-life-thesis.md) survive contact with two businesses._

---

## The problem this solves

The current default is **reactive allocation**: the week fills with whatever
escalates loudest — an incident, a competitor announcement, a support edge case,
a Formaviva approval. Reactive allocation always produces the same outcome:
maintenance wins, growth loses, health loses, and the loved project becomes
guilt. It also guarantees that the founder stays on the critical path, because
being reachable is the same thing as being required.

The fix is not more discipline. It is **structural**: decide in advance what
each part of the week is for, so that the default answer to an interruption is
already written down.

---

## The three modes

Most of the exhaustion comes from running all three simultaneously.

| Mode | What it is | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| **Maker** | Strategy, writing, product thinking, deep problem-solving | Long uninterrupted blocks, morning, no Slack |
| **Manager** | Demos, 1:1s, customer calls, decisions, unblocking | Batched into defined windows, afternoons |
| **Owner** | Reviewing numbers, hiring, endgame planning, portfolio calls | A protected, recurring slot — the first thing to be cannibalized, the most valuable |

**Owner mode is the one currently missing**, and it is the one that removes the
founder from the critical path. Everything in
[`06-nightwatch-adjustments.md`](06-nightwatch-adjustments.md) requires it. A week
with zero Owner hours is a week that guarantees next quarter looks like this one.

## The week template

A starting shape, not a prescription. Adjust the times; keep the structure.

| | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Morning (deep)** | Weekly review + Owner block | Maker | Maker | Maker | Maker |
| **Midday** | Movement / walk / lunch | ← | ← | ← | ← |
| **Afternoon (batched)** | Manager: demos, calls | Manager | Manager | Manager | Friday close, then **stop** |
| **Evening** | Off | Off | Off | Off | Off |

**Saturday or Sunday: one genuinely off day. Non-negotiable.**
**One bounded Formaviva slot per week** — a fixed afternoon, everything for
Formaviva happens inside it (see [`07`](07-formaviva-adjustments.md)).

### The rules that make the template real

1. **Mornings are not for meetings.** The best cognitive hours currently go to
   whoever booked first. Nothing recurring goes before midday except the Monday
   review.
2. **Meetings batch into afternoons.** The MRR plan calls for 5–8 demos/week —
   they belong in two or three afternoon blocks, not spread across five days
   destroying five mornings.
3. **One context per day where possible.** Day-level separation, not hour-level.
   The re-entry cost between B2B SaaS and music curation is paid twice per
   switch.
4. **Evenings end.** A defined stop time, with notifications off. See
   [`03-energy-and-health.md`](03-energy-and-health.md).
5. **Slack and email are batched into windows**, not run continuously. Async is
   already a company value — same-day, not same-minute.

## The Monday review (30 minutes, whole week depends on it)

Short, mechanical, always the same. Sequence matters — floor before business.

1. **Floor check (5 min).** Sleep anchor held? Movement done? Off day taken?
   Anything on the personal dashboard trending wrong? _If the floor is broken,
   fix the week before planning it._
2. **Numbers (10 min).** Open `MRR-EXECUTION-TRACKER.md`. Fill actuals. Look at
   the leading indicators before the MRR number — they predict it.
3. **The one thing (5 min).** What single outcome, if achieved this week, makes
   the week a success? Write it down. Everything else is negotiable.
4. **Delegation pass (5 min).** What is on your list this week that should
   belong to someone else, permanently? Move one item per week. Over a quarter
   that is 12 items off the critical path — that is the de-bottlenecking goal,
   executed in five minutes a week.
5. **Protect the blocks (5 min).** Movement, deep work, the Formaviva slot, the
   off day. If they are not defended on Monday, they will be gone by Wednesday.

## The Friday close (15 minutes)

1. **What shipped?** Facts, not effort.
2. **What did not, and why?** Blocked, deprioritized, or just too much? "Too
   much" three weeks running is a capacity signal, and the response is
   subtraction.
3. **What is now safe to delete?** Every week should remove something. This is
   the Finishability value made operational.
4. **Update the tracker. Close the laptop.** The close is what makes the weekend
   actually off — an un-closed week runs in the background for two days.

## Attention protection

### Deep work

- **Two protected blocks per week minimum**, 3+ hours each, calendar-blocked
  and defended. Citation Intelligence decisions, positioning, hiring specs, and
  the endgame document all require this mode and none of them can be done in
  30-minute gaps.
- **Phone in another room.** Not face down — another room.
- **One tab, one task.** The strategy documents in `nightwatch-org` were written
  in this mode; the execution gaps in `CONCLUSIONS.md` persist because execution
  has been attempted in the gaps between meetings.

### Interruption budget

Interruptions are not free and should be counted like any other cost:

| Source | Current default | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Sentry / uptime alerts | Reaches founder | On-call engineer; founder by human escalation only |
| Slack DMs | Instant, all hours | Batched windows; no after-hours notifications |
| Customer escalations | Founder-attached | CS-owned, with a defined escalation bar |
| Formaviva approvals | Ad hoc, whenever | Inside the weekly Formaviva slot only |
| Competitive news | Continuous | Monthly review block |

**Every row moved from left to right is a permanent reduction in load** — not a
one-time saving. That is why the delegation pass is weekly and mandatory.

---

## Calendar hygiene

- **Default meeting length 25/50 minutes**, not 30/60. Transitions are where the
  day is lost.
- **No meetings before midday.** Stated publicly so nobody has to ask.
- **A recurring "no meetings" day** if it can be defended — even one is
  transformative for Maker and Owner work.
- **Buffers around demos.** Five back-to-back demos with no gaps is how an
  afternoon becomes an evening.
- **Health blocks have the same status as customer calls.** They are not moved
  for a meeting; a meeting is moved for them. This is the decision hierarchy made
  visible in a calendar — and other people learn what is real by watching what
  gets moved.

## Seasons, not steady state

A year is not uniform, and pretending otherwise is how sprints become the
permanent condition:

- **Sprint seasons** (like the current 90-day MRR push) run hotter — accepted,
  time-boxed, with an end date that is honored.
- **Recovery seasons** follow them. After the 90-day sprint closes, the next
  block is consolidation and rest, not immediately the next sprint. If sprint
  follows sprint indefinitely, the word "sprint" has stopped meaning anything and
  the body will eventually enforce the recovery season involuntarily.
- **Formaviva may get a season** — a deliberate, time-boxed, funded push once
  Nightwatch's endgame resolves. That is the honest way to give it a real chance,
  rather than starving it continuously.

**Write the season on the calendar.** Knowing that a hard quarter has a defined
end is the difference between a sprint and a grind, and it costs nothing.

# Longer cadences

_Merged from the former `11-review-cadence.md`. A strategy that is not revised
against reality is fiction — and nobody else will notice when a personal
operating system drifts._

| Cadence | Duration | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Monday review** | 30 min | Set the week; floor check first, then numbers (above) |
| **Friday close** | 15 min | What shipped, what to delete, close the week (above) |
| **Monthly** | 60 min | Trends, competitive review, one mandatory subtraction |
| **Quarterly** | half day | Goals, values, scope, seasons |
| **Annual** | one day | Endgame, portfolio, health baseline, life shape |

**Total: about 1.5% of a working year.** If it feels like more, it has become a
project — the failure mode this repo warns about throughout.

## The monthly review (60 minutes, first Monday)

1. **Floor trend (10 min).** Four weeks of the five-line dashboard. Is energy
   trending up or down? Is the sleep anchor holding? Trend matters; any single
   week does not.
2. **Business numbers (15 min).** MRR tracker plus the cost/reliability page.
   Are the leading indicators moving before the lagging ones?
3. **Competitive review (20 min).** The one scheduled competitive block of the
   month. One document. Then closed until next month.
4. **Delegation audit (10 min).** What landed back on the founder's plate this
   month, and why? Recurring items are systems problems, not discipline problems.
5. **The subtraction (5 min).** **What gets deleted this month?** Mandatory —
   every month removes something: a commitment, a doc, a tool, a meeting, a
   feature, an obligation. A month with no subtraction is a month of accumulation.

---

## The quarterly review (half day, protected)

Run against [`01-life-thesis.md`](01-life-thesis.md), not against the business
plan.

1. **Goals.** Re-read Horizon 1 and 2. Which are done, which are dead? **Delete
   dead goals rather than carrying them as guilt** — a stale goal list is a
   standing accusation.
2. **Values check.** Did Finishability, Deliberate Smallness, and Floor Before
   Ceiling actually govern any decision this quarter? If not, they are decoration
   and need enforcement mechanisms, not better intentions.
3. **Scope audit.** What surface was added? What was removed? **Net surface
   should be flat or negative.** This is the single most diagnostic number in the
   whole review.
4. **Founder-footprint audit.** How many things still require you personally?
   Is it fewer than last quarter? This is the de-bottlenecking scoreboard.
5. **Season check.** Was this a sprint or a recovery season? Was the next one
   chosen deliberately — or did a sprint simply continue by default? Two sprint
   seasons back to back is a warning; three is a health event waiting to happen.
6. **Formaviva cap review.** Was the cap held? Is the decision date still right?
   Has anything changed that reopens the question?
7. **Update the docs.** Anything proven wrong gets rewritten with a date. This
   repo is a living record, not a monument.

---

## The annual review (one day, away from the desk)

1. **Health baseline.** Annual physical and bloodwork. Compare to last year.
   Trend, not snapshot.
2. **Financial floor.** Recompute personal runway, burn, exposure, and the
   "enough" number. Has the floor risen?
3. **The endgame document.** Re-read [`08`](08-portfolio-strategy.md). Is the
   answer still true? It is allowed to change — but it should change
   deliberately, in a quiet room, not under offer pressure.
4. **Portfolio.** Are both ventures still in the right mode? Is Formaviva's
   decision date reached or approaching?
5. **The life question.** Not a business question: _is this life better than
   last year's?_ If the P&L improved and the answer is no, the strategy is
   working and the thesis is wrong — and the thesis is what should change.
6. **Relationships.** Who got real time this year? Who did not, and should have?
   This is the item most likely to be skipped and most likely to matter in ten
   years.

## Signals to watch for

Early-warning indicators that the system is drifting back to the default state.
Each has a pre-written response so that noticing it does not require a decision.

| Signal | What it means | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Energy ≤2 for 3+ weeks | Load exceeds capacity | Subtract immediately — pre-decided in [`08`](08-portfolio-strategy.md) |
| Reviews being skipped | The system has become overhead | Shorten the reviews, don't abandon them |
| New surface added with nothing removed | Gate 3 is being bypassed | Reinstate the subtraction rule; audit what was added |
| Founder-owned levers increasing | Delegation reversing | Weekly delegation pass in the Monday review |
| Formaviva exceeding its cap | The cap was aspirational, not real | Re-cap, or bring the decision date forward |
| A rewrite being contemplated | The craft-over-focus failure mode | Gate 2: does it raise the floor or the ceiling? |
| "I'll rest after this quarter" (twice) | Sprint has become the permanent condition | Book the recovery season on the calendar now |
| Competitive news being checked daily | Monitoring boundary broken | Back to the monthly block |
| Numbers being estimated in decisions | Baseline has gone stale | Re-pull from source |

**The last two rows are the most common relapses**, because both feel like
diligence rather than drift.

## What good looks like in 12 months

Not revenue targets — those live in the business repos. These are the
operator-level outcomes that determine whether the revenue targets are reachable
at all, and reachable again the year after:

- The wake anchor is boring and automatic.
- A physical has been taken, with a trend to compare against.
- Alerts do not reach the founder by default, and nobody misses them.
- Growth has an owner who is not the founder.
- Cost per check is a visible number that someone owns and is trending down.
- Competitive research happens monthly and generates no anxiety.
- Formaviva is alive, capped, secure, loved, and has a decision date that was
  actually met.
- The endgame document exists and was written calmly.
- Personal runway is measured, and larger than it was.
- There was a real holiday. There is a weekly off day. There is one thing done
  purely for enjoyment with no output attached.
- **Net surface area is lower than it was 12 months ago.**

That last one is the whole thesis in a single line. If it is true, everything
else in this repo worked.
