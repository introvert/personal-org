# People & Relationships

> ⚠️ **This is the thinnest-evidenced document in the repo, and deliberately so.**
> Everything else here was built from source documents in `nightwatch-org` and
> `formaviva-org`. There are no source documents about your life. So this doc is
> **scaffolding and prompts, not conclusions** — the sections marked _"fill in"_
> are genuinely for you, and no AI should invent them on your behalf.

---

## Why this exists

[`01-life-thesis.md`](01-life-thesis.md) puts relationships on the **floor
tier** — above growth, above upside, second only to health and integrity. Then
the rest of the repo gave them a single line.

That gap is not an oversight in the writing. **It is an accurate reproduction of
the problem.** Relationships are the thing everyone ranks highest and schedules
last.

## The structural reason this happens

Everything else in your life sends alerts.

A server goes down and Sentry pages someone. Revenue slips and the tracker shows
it. A customer is unhappy and a ticket appears. Even your body eventually
escalates — badly, but it escalates.

> **Relationships have no monitoring. They degrade silently, over years, with no
> alert, and by the time the signal is loud enough to notice, the degradation is
> the accumulated kind that took a decade to build and takes a decade to undo.**

This is why "I'll have more time after this quarter" is more dangerous here than
anywhere else in this repo. Every other system in your life will interrupt you
when it is failing. This one won't.

**The design consequence:** relationships need *scheduled* attention precisely
because they will never demand it. Everything unscheduled loses to everything
that pages you.

## Design rules

Few, because a relationship system with rules nobody follows is worse than none.

**1. Recurring beats intentional.** A standing weekly call, a monthly dinner, an
annual trip — these survive bad quarters. "We should catch up" does not. Convert
the two or three relationships that matter most into recurring commitments and
stop relying on intent.

**2. Calendar priority, not residual time.** The decision hierarchy already says
relationships outrank growth. The calendar is where that becomes real or doesn't
— and other people learn what is actually true by watching what gets moved.

**3. Presence, not availability.** Being in the room with a phone in reach is
not presence, and the people close to you can tell the difference precisely.
This is the same boundary work as
[`03-energy-and-health.md`](03-energy-and-health.md), pointed at people instead
of sleep.

**4. One annual audit.** Already in the annual review: _who got real time this
year, and who should have?_ It is the item most likely to be skipped and most
likely to matter in ten years.

## The three relationship situations specific to you

These are the ones a generic document would miss, and they are the reason this
doc is worth having at all.

### 1. Your team is three people, and you are their employer

At ~11 people you are a manager. **At ~3 you are something closer to a
colleague, and probably the single most significant professional relationship in
each of their working lives.** That cuts both ways:

- **The delegation this repo recommends is also a relationship act.** Handing
  someone real ownership is trust made concrete, and it usually strengthens the
  relationship rather than straining it. Withholding ownership while being
  overloaded reads, from the outside, as not trusting them.
- **A liquidity event affects them more than it affects most employees**, because
  the company is a larger share of their working world. What happens to them in
  each endgame scenario belongs in the endgame document
  ([`08`](08-portfolio-strategy.md)) — under "what you will not trade," not as an
  afterthought.
- **Bus factor 1 is a people problem as much as a technical one.** Somebody
  carrying a system alone cannot take a proper holiday. Fixing redundancy is also
  giving them their time back.

### 2. Capping Formaviva has a relational cost — pay it explicitly

The cap ([`07`](07-formaviva-adjustments.md)) is the right call, but Formaviva is
not just a codebase. It is **artists who trusted you personally**, a scene where
your name means something, and relationships built over years.

> **A cap communicated is a decision. A cap not communicated is going quiet —
> and going quiet is how trust actually gets lost.**

Nobody in the scene will be upset that the roadmap slowed. People *will* notice
if messages stop being answered and applications sit for months with no reply.

**Concretely:** tell the people who matter what the position is — that Formaviva
is continuing, that curation and radio continue, that platform work is paused,
and roughly when you'll revisit. Two paragraphs. It converts an unexplained
slowdown into a stated position, and it costs one afternoon inside the weekly
slot.

### 3. Peer founders — the relationship category most likely missing

Thirteen years in, pre-diligence, with two ventures and a small team, there is a
specific kind of conversation you can only have with someone who has been
through it: another founder, ideally one who has been through an exit.

This is not networking. It is the **only external check on the closed loop**
named in [`02-current-state.md`](02-current-state.md) — everything in this repo
was derived from documents written inside your own organizations, and a peer
founder is the cheapest available way to find the blind spot they share.

**One conversation before the endgame document is written would improve it more
than another week of analysis.** If there is nobody obvious, that itself is
worth knowing, and it is fixable.

## The failure mode to watch for

> **"After the exit, I'll have time."**

This is the single most common founder promise and the least reliable. Every
outcome creates its own next demand — an earnout, a new venture, a raise, or
simply the discovery that unstructured time is harder than expected. The people
in your life have been hearing a version of this for thirteen years.

**Nothing in this document requires waiting for an outcome.** A standing weekly
call costs nothing and starts this week. That is the entire point of putting it
on the floor tier rather than in the plan.
