# Money & "Enough"

> ⚠️ **Not financial or tax advice.** Structure, tax, and anything touching a
> transaction need a qualified advisor who knows Slovenian and EU rules and can
> see your actual affairs. This document is about **the decisions and the
> numbers you need in front of you** before those conversations are useful.
>
> Like [`10`](10-people-and-relationships.md), the sections marked _"fill in"_
> are genuinely yours — no figures here are invented on your behalf.

---

## Why this is a separate document

[`08-portfolio-strategy.md`](08-portfolio-strategy.md) covers the financial floor
in half a page, as an input to business strategy. That was the wrong altitude.

Money is not a business topic here. It is the variable that determines **whether
decisions get made from patience or from fear** — and with a diligence process
ahead, that distinction is about to matter more than it ever has.

The business is in good shape financially: ~78% operating margin, ~$46,700/month
of surplus ([`02`](02-current-state.md)). **What is unknown is your position, not
the company's.** Those are different balance sheets, and conflating them is how
founders end up feeling poor while running a profitable business — or the
reverse.

## The four numbers _(fill in — one afternoon)_

Nearly everyone in your position knows the company's numbers precisely and their
own only vaguely.

| # | Number | Definition | Yours |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Personal burn** | What the household actually costs per month | |
| 2 | **Personal runway** | Months covered by personal assets alone, with **zero** income from either business | |
| 3 | **Exposure** | Loans, guarantees, deferred salary, personal liability, anything that turns a business problem into a personal one | |
| 4 | **"Enough"** | The post-tax figure at which work becomes genuinely optional | |

**Target for #2: 12–24 months held entirely outside both businesses.** If it
isn't there, building it is a legitimate top-tier goal, not a distraction from
growth.

### Why #4 is the one that matters most

Almost nobody computes "enough," and the consequences are predictable:

- **You cannot recognize a good offer without it.** With no number, every offer
  is evaluated against "could it be more?" — and the answer is always yes.
- **Without it, more is the only direction.** A target you never defined is a
  target you can never hit, which is how people spend a second decade earning
  past the point where it changes anything.
- **It is the input the endgame document depends on.** #14 in
  [`PLAN.md`](../PLAN.md) asks for a walk-away number. **This is that number**,
  and it has to be computed before it can be walked away from.

Compute it plainly: annual cost of the life you actually want × the years you
want it covered, plus a margin, adjusted for what you'd realistically keep
earning. It is arithmetic, not philosophy — the hard part is being honest about
the life you actually want, which is [`13`](13-life-design.md).

## What money is for

Worth stating, because in practice it silently defaults to a scoreboard.

> **Money's job here is to convert into time, optionality, and security —
> nothing else.** Every euro that does not buy one of those three is doing no
> work.

Applied to actual decisions in this repo:

- **Runway buys patience**, and patience is the highest-value asset in any
  negotiation. Someone with 24 months of personal runway negotiates differently
  from someone with three, and counterparties can tell.
- **The ~$46,700/month surplus buys optionality** — a growth owner is 6–11% of
  it. **Money was never the constraint on fixing the bottleneck.** That is worth
  sitting with, because the constraint was something else.
- **Being bootstrapped and profitable is the rarest asset you have.** No investor
  clock, no board, no forced timeline. **Spend that on optionality, not on
  proving something.** Most founders never get this and cannot buy it back.
- **The Formaviva cap is a purchase.** A fixed monthly figure buying a thing you
  love, guilt-free inside the cap — like a studio rental or a boat. Priced and
  chosen, it stops being a failing business and becomes a paid-for good.

## Ahead of a transaction

Only the parts that are yours to prepare. Everything else is for an advisor.

**Know your structure before anyone asks.** Corporate structure, share
structure, who else holds equity, employee agreements, IP ownership, any
outstanding obligations. This is listed as an open unknown in
[`02`](02-current-state.md) and it cannot be answered mid-process.

**Get the tax picture early, not at signing.** Slovenian and EU treatment of a
transaction can differ substantially by structure and timing, and some options
close once a process starts. **This is the single highest-value professional
conversation available to you right now**, and it is one meeting.

**Separate price from terms.** Founders anchor on headline price; the terms —
earnout length, retention obligations, what happens to your three people,
reporting lines — determine what the next two years of your life actually feel
like. **A lower number with clean terms is frequently the better outcome.** Write
your position on terms into the endgame document *before* you see a number,
because afterwards it is nearly impossible to think about them independently.

**Decide what you will not trade.** Health during the process. Your team's
treatment. Honesty in the data room. Time with the people in
[`10`](10-people-and-relationships.md) during the closing period. Each of these
will come under pressure; each is much easier to hold if it was written down
first.

## After

The scenario least planned for, and the one where money most often goes wrong.

- **Lifestyle inflation is the classic error.** Fixed costs rise to meet the new
  number, and "enough" quietly moves out of reach again. The number you computed
  in #4 is only useful if it stays fixed after the event.
- **A liquidity event changes relationships.** Money alters dynamics with family,
  friends, and former colleagues in ways that are hard to anticipate and
  awkward to discuss afterwards. Worth thinking about beforehand — see
  [`10`](10-people-and-relationships.md).
- **The identity question arrives faster than the money problem.**
  See [`13-life-design.md`](13-life-design.md). Founders who sell are far more
  often blindsided by "who am I now" than by anything financial.
- **Get advice before, not after.** Structuring options narrow sharply once a
  transaction completes.

## What not to do

- ❌ **Don't turn personal finance into a project.** Spreadsheets, portfolio
  optimization, and tracking systems are the same craft-over-focus trap as
  everything else in this repo. Four numbers, once, updated annually.
- ❌ **Don't let the company's health substitute for knowing your own.** A
  profitable business is not a personal balance sheet.
- ❌ **Don't defer #4 until an offer exists.** That is precisely when it becomes
  impossible to compute honestly.
- ❌ **Don't confuse a good business outcome with a good life outcome.** The
  annual review asks whether this life is better than last year's. The P&L cannot
  answer that question and should not be asked to.

---

## The one-line version

> **Compute four numbers once. Hold runway so decisions come from patience
> rather than fear. Define "enough" before someone else offers you a number —
> because after that, you will never be able to compute it honestly again.**
