# Portfolio Strategy — Two Ventures, One Life, One Endgame

_How Nightwatch and Formaviva fit together, what the personal financial floor
needs to be, and the single most important document that has not yet been
written._

---

## The portfolio as it actually is

| | **Nightwatch** | **Formaviva** |
|---|---|---|
| Role | The engine — income, team, obligation, the asset with real value | The soul — identity, creative renewal, the loved work |
| Stage | Mature, profitable, repositioning, pre-diligence | Pre-revenue-model, capped |
| Time horizon | **Urgent** — the AI-search window is time-sensitive | **Patient** — the scene moat does not depreciate quickly |
| Founder role | Should be shrinking (owner, not operator) | Should be bounded (one slot/week) |
| Optionality | The outcome that funds everything else | The option that outcome buys back |

**The relationship between them is the strategic insight:** Nightwatch is not
competing with Formaviva for attention — **Nightwatch is the mechanism by which
Formaviva eventually gets a real chance.** A good Nightwatch outcome buys money,
time, and freedom. Attention taken from Nightwatch and given to Formaviva now
lowers the probability of the outcome that would fund Formaviva properly later.

Stated plainly: **the fastest route to Formaviva succeeding runs through
Nightwatch, not around it.** That reframe converts the cap from a sacrifice into
a strategy, and it is the reason [`07`](07-formaviva-adjustments.md) recommends
capping rather than pushing.

## The asymmetry that determines sequencing

| Dimension | Nightwatch | Formaviva |
|---|---|---|
| Window | The AI-search category is being defined **now**; Peec is growing fast; `EXECUTIVE-SUMMARY.md` estimates ~12 months before Semrush/Ahrefs close the gap | Underground electronic music is not being disrupted on a 12-month clock |
| Cost of a year's delay | **High** — possibly the category | **Low** — the scene and relationships persist |
| Resources required now | Founder attention, engineering, focus | A cap |
| Downside of underinvesting | 800 customers, 11 salaries, a 13-year asset | A capped project stays capped |

The sequencing follows mechanically: **Nightwatch first, on a deadline;
Formaviva protected, on a date.** This is not a ranking of what matters more as
a human. It is a ranking of what is time-sensitive.

## The personal financial floor (do this before the endgame gets real)

The most underrated health and decision-quality intervention available, and it
takes one afternoon.

**Compute, on one page:**

1. **Personal runway** — months of personal expenses covered by personal assets
   alone, with zero income from either business.
2. **Personal burn** — what the household actually costs per month.
3. **Personal exposure to the businesses** — loans, guarantees, deferred salary,
   anything that makes a business problem into a personal one.
4. **The "enough" number** — what post-tax outcome would make work genuinely
   optional. Most founders have never written this down, which is exactly why
   they cannot recognize a good offer when it arrives.

**Why this matters more than it sounds:** every negotiation, every hiring
decision, every "should I take this deal" is answered differently by someone with
24 months of personal runway than by someone with three. Fear is a strategy tax,
and it is paid invisibly, in worse decisions. **Runway converts fear into
patience, and patience is the highest-value asset in any negotiation.**

**Target:** 12–24 months of personal runway held entirely outside both
businesses. If it is not there, building it is a legitimate top-tier goal, not a
distraction from growth.

## The endgame document (the highest-value unwritten document)

`nightwatch-org` has ~30 strategy documents. **None of them answers: what does
the founder actually want out of this?**

Diligence is ahead of a launch. That means a process is coming that will
generate offers, pressure, timelines, advisors, and a great deal of noise —
and it will ask the question in a form that demands an immediate answer.

> **Write the answer before the process makes it urgent. Decisions made under
> offer pressure are systematically worse than the same decisions made six
> months earlier in a quiet room.**

### What the endgame document must contain

1. **What outcome are you actually optimizing for?** Be specific and honest:
   - Maximum valuation?
   - Freedom and time, sooner?
   - The team landing well?
   - The product continuing to exist as itself?
   - Staying on to run it at scale — genuinely, or from obligation?
   These trade against each other, and pretending otherwise is how founders end
   up with an outcome that is objectively good and personally wrong.

2. **The walk-away number and the walk-away terms.** Not just price — earnout
   length, retention obligations, reporting lines, what happens to the team.
   Written down in advance, so the number cannot be rationalized upward or
   downward in the moment.

3. **What you will not trade.** Health during the process. The team's treatment.
   Honesty in the data room. Time with family during the closing period. Name
   them now, because each will come under pressure.

4. **Life after each scenario.** Concretely: what does the year after a sale
   look like? The year after continuing? The year after a raise? If a scenario
   has no attractive answer, that is important information about the scenario —
   and the most common founder regret is not price, it is discovering the answer
   only afterward.

5. **What it means for Formaviva.** Each scenario maps to a branch in
   [`07`](07-formaviva-adjustments.md): fund it properly, run it as media, or
   hand it over. Deciding this in advance removes the largest emotional variable
   from the negotiation.

**Schedule it as a dedicated Owner block** ([`04`](04-time-and-attention.md)).
Not squeezed between demos. This document is worth more than any of the 30
strategy documents, because every one of those assumes an answer to it.

## Capital allocation across the portfolio

The current implicit allocation, made by default rather than by decision:

```
Nightwatch:  ~all money, ~most attention, all urgency
Formaviva:   whatever is left of both, plus the guilt
Personal:    the residual — which in practice is near zero
```

The proposed explicit allocation:

```
Nightwatch:  money and attention, capped by a sustainable pace and a
             shrinking founder footprint
Formaviva:   a fixed monthly cap, one slot per week, security-only engineering
Personal:    a protected floor — health blocks, an off day, real holidays,
             and a personal runway that is being deliberately built
```

**The change is not in the ratios. It is that all three lines are now
deliberate, capped, and reviewable.** An unspecified allocation always resolves
toward whatever escalates loudest, which is how the personal line reached zero
without anyone ever deciding it should.

## Scenario map

Written in advance so no scenario arrives as a surprise, and so the response to
each is a plan rather than a reaction.

| Scenario | Probability signal | Response |
|---|---|---|
| **Diligence → good outcome** | Number hit, clean metrics, real interest | Execute the endgame document. Fund or hand over Formaviva per its branch. Take real recovery time before the next thing |
| **Diligence → no deal, business continues** | Process ends without terms worth taking | Fine — profitable and bootstrapped. Pivot to the de-bottlenecking agenda: hire growth, hire delivery, reduce founder footprint permanently. This is the scenario the 90-day plan strengthens either way |
| **Nightwatch stalls** | 90-day target missed by a wide margin; churn up; conversion flat | Do **not** add effort — that is the trap. Re-read [`05`](05-decision-framework.md): floor before ceiling, subtract before adding. Cut surface, protect the base, extend the timeline |
| **Health event** | Energy ≤2 for 3+ consecutive weeks, or a medical finding | **Immediate subtraction.** Formaviva pauses fully. Nightwatch drops to essential operations. This is pre-decided so it does not require deciding while depleted |
| **Formaviva opportunity** | A partner, buyer, or co-founder appears | Evaluate through Gates 1–5. A genuine partner is the single best outcome for it — it gets a real chance without taking founder capacity |

**Sole-founder note.** There is no co-founder in either venture. Every scenario
above is yours to decide alone, and — more importantly — **yours to survive
alone.** That makes the health-event row below not merely prudent but the single
most under-insured risk in the portfolio: there is no partner to hold anything
while you recover. It is also the concentration risk a buyer will price most
heavily, which is a further argument for the owner-level hires in
[`06`](06-nightwatch-adjustments.md) — they are the only redundancy available.

**The health-event row is the most important one on this page,** because it is
the only scenario where good judgment is guaranteed to be unavailable at the
moment the decision is needed. Pre-deciding it is the entire point.
